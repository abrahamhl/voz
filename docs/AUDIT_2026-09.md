# VOZ product audit

Date: 2026-09-24
Scope: Android code, release pipeline, privacy/security controls, accessibility
claims, Google Play preparation and product evidence.

## Executive decision

VOZ is not yet ready for a public Play launch or an investment claim that the
product is proven. It is a promising release candidate with a strong local-first
and safety-oriented thesis. The blockers are evidence and real-device behavior,
not a missing marketing layer.

## Closed in this audit

- Password and secret accessibility nodes are excluded from screen snapshots,
  spoken screen reading, cloud fencing and clickable-container risk labels.
- Any confirmed tap, including a cloud-inferred non-sensitive tap, is re-resolved
  after confirmation and rejected if the target moved, changed labels or became
  ambiguous.
- Sensitive-action detection now covers common authorization and workflow labels
  such as Submit, Continue, Next, Finish, Save, Authorize, Verify and their
  Spanish/Dutch equivalents.
- Local fuzzy tapping now requires an explicit action verb unless the spoken text
  exactly matches the visible label, and it requires a margin over the runner-up.
- Persistent recognized speech is now length-only, including successful commands
  and stop phrases, instead of storing arbitrary private text verbatim.
- History writes use temporary-file plus atomic rename instead of overwriting the
  audit file in place.
- Privacy and Play drafts no longer claim that the manifest attribute is Play
  approval or that no data can leave the device through Android speech providers
  and destination apps.
- Tests cover secret-field exclusion, new sensitive labels, local-planner false
  positives and history temporary-file cleanup.

## Remaining launch gates

1. Run the complete JVM and Android test suite in clean CI and publish exact
   counts tied to a commit and CI run URL.
2. Build and inspect the signed APK and AAB from that exact commit. Verify merged
   manifest, no `INTERNET`, cloud flag false and no cloud/OkHttp classes.
3. Validate on at least two physical phones, including Android 13+ and one
   TalkBack run; add a non-stock OEM if possible.
4. Test password managers, browser/WebView content, messaging, payment-like
   flows, duplicate labels, TalkBack coexistence, lock/unlock and service restart.
5. Test with blind, low-vision and motor-impaired participants before claiming
   usability or product-market fit.
6. Recheck current Google Play AccessibilityService, User Data, microphone
   foreground-service and Data Safety requirements against the exact AAB.
7. Publish a stable HTTPS privacy policy and complete the Play declaration and
   Data Safety form. The internal draft is not approval evidence.
8. Resolve trademark, legal-entity, DPA and Gemini pilot terms with qualified
   counsel before offering cloud planning to consumers.

## Product position

The defensible near-term proposition is not “a general AI agent”. It is a
conservative, offline-first accessibility control layer that turns spoken intent
into bounded cross-app actions, exposes confirmation and failure states, and
keeps experimental cloud planning out of the public release. The differentiator
becomes investable only after task-completion, false-activation, unsafe-action
prevention and recovery metrics are measured against Voice Access/TalkBack on
real devices with intended users.
